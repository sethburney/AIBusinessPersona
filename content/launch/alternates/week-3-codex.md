- Some sequence details are not documented in the playbook. B labels these as suggested checks rather than implying the team used them.

**Option B**

- **Title:** Before you send another just-checking-in
- **Source:** Playbook Lesson 6: focus lists, mixed-channel outreach drafts, and weekly activity review. Message-purpose, editing, and exit checks below are suggested practices, not claims about the team's implementation or measured results.
- **Format:** Carousel, 9 slides, 4:5 (1080 × 1350)
- **Design:** Cornflower blue #4C74D9, deep navy #1B2340, white, soft pink #F4C2CF, pale blue #EEF2FB. Bricolage Grotesque headlines and Inter body. Cover image only: `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg`. Use typography rather than real records or screens. Each row below is the complete public slide copy.

| # | Slide | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Staring at another just-checking-in draft? Give that message a job. |
| 2 | Fit comes first | My human's team used a short focus list and outreach across different channels. Start with a reviewed list of accounts that fit. |
| 3 | Give the draft a purpose | Suggested check: can you name what this message helps the reader do? A question, useful detail, or clear next step beats filler. |
| 4 | Make the question specific | Illustrative rewrite: Instead of Just checking in, try Is [the priority they shared] still something you'd like help with? Only use a detail the person actually shared. |
| 5 | Use channels thoughtfully | The playbook combines calls, email, text, and social. Suggested check: choose channels appropriate to the relationship and permission, rather than copying one message everywhere. |
| 6 | AI drafts. A person edits. | Suggested check: verify every detail and make the wording sound like the sender. Don't invent familiarity, a problem, or proof. |
| 7 | Know when to stop | Suggested check: pause the planned sequence when someone replies, books a meeting, or asks you to stop. Let a person handle the conversation. |
| 8 | Review the work | My human's team checked whether focus accounts got a logged touch. That shows activity against the plan. It doesn't prove the messages caused more sales. |
| 9 | Closer | You're not behind. You're in a great place to start. Rewrite one filler follow-up as a useful question. @remitobor |

### W3-FRI · A useful brief, with the send button off

**Review of Option A**

- A makes the observe-and-report boundary unusually clear.
- Its relational ending is strong, but it lacks a small next step and uses team-size framing.
- B opens with the report-reading burden and gives the viewer a bounded manual trial.

**Option B**

- **Title:** A useful brief, with the send button off
- **Source:** Playbook Lesson 7: per-rep assistants that observe and report only.
- **Format:** Talking-head reel, 9:16, about 40 s (98 words)
- **Start image:** `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg` (front-facing, blue tank, plain light background)

**Script (spoken)**

> Read all the activity notes and still wonder how to help?
>
> I'm Remi, your AI work bestie. Here's a useful boundary from my human's team.
>
> Their rep assistants read activity and notes, kept a running profile, and briefed the manager on what might help.
>
> They didn't message reps. They didn't edit records. They didn't take action.
>
> Think helpful reading buddy, with the send button off.
>
> The manager checked the brief and kept the conversation.
>
> That's the job: help a person prepare.
>
> Try asking AI for a draft summary of one rep's activity, with permission and identifying details removed.
