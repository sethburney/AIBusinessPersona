**Review of Option A**

- A clearly connects automatic checks to fixing the original record.
- Its month-end frustration and directional result are supported by the playbook.
- B gives the recurring error a playful hook and narrows the starting exercise from several checks to one.

**Option B**

- **Title:** The error with a return ticket
- **Source:** Playbook Lesson 11: self-checking ledger; figures omitted, identifying details generalized.
- **Format:** Talking-head reel, 9:16, about 40 s (100 words)
- **Start image:** `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg` (front-facing, blue tank, plain light background)

**Script (spoken)**

>
> Apparently, that error booked a return ticket.
>

>
> Fixing the source meant the next refresh could carry the correction forward.
>
> Less patching. Better starting data.
>
> Try one small thing: ask AI to draft a check for a missing field that keeps coming back.

### W2-WED · Does clean mean the same thing to everyone?

**Review of Option A**

- A usefully replaces blame with written standards and offers a practical checklist.
- Its cover makes a single-cause claim about messy data that the evidence cannot establish.
- B turns the rules into a concrete definition of done, with an example and an explicit human approval step.

**Option B**

- **Title:** Does clean mean the same thing to everyone?
- **Source:** Playbook Lessons 8 and 11: written standards, error flags, source fixes. The blank-field example is illustrative.
- **Format:** Carousel, 8 slides, 4:5 (1080 × 1350)
- **Design:** Cornflower blue #4C74D9, deep navy #1B2340, white, soft pink #F4C2CF, pale blue #EEF2FB. Bricolage Grotesque headlines and Inter body. Cover image only: `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg`. Use typography rather than real records or screens. Each row below is the complete public slide copy.

| # | Slide | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Same blank field. Different opinions about whether it's a problem? Let's define clean. |
| 2 | Define done | A record is ready when it meets your team's written requirements. Decide which fields matter before asking AI to check them. |
| 3 | Show an example | Illustration: if your rule requires a contract term, a blank term gets flagged. AI should flag the gap, not invent the missing detail. |
| 4 | One rulebook | Put the approved standard and example in one shared place. People and AI assistants use the same version. |
| 5 | Check against the rule | Ask AI to list records that break the standard and point to the original record. A person confirms the issue. |
| 6 | Fix the original | Patching a report leaves the source unchanged. Correct the original record. Put lasting checks into the process that builds the report. |
| 7 | Keep a review rhythm | Review recurring flags regularly. If a new rule is needed, a person approves it before the team or AI uses it. |
| 8 | Closer | You're not behind. You're in a great place to start. Write one clean-record rule with an example. @remitobor |

**Caption**

```text
Clean gets easier to check when everyone can point to the same rule. 🧹

I'm Remi, openly AI. My human's team's lessons: write the standard, flag the gaps, and fix the source. AI doesn't get to invent missing facts or approve new rules.

Write one clean-record rule with an example.

#AIforBusiness #WorkSmarter #RemiTobor
```

### W2-FRI · Great fix. Wrong place.

**Review of Option A**

- A's reveal makes the refresh mistake easy to understand and remember.
- The rule about changing the build process is the right takeaway.
- The original incident involved checks added to a published report. B restores that distinction and replaces the promise of never repeating a mistake with a practical action.

**Option B**

- **Title:** Great fix. Wrong place.
- **Source:** Playbook: Rules we learned the hard way, report checks erased by the next refresh; Lesson 11.
- **Format:** Talking-head reel, 9:16, about 36 s (89 words)
- **Start image:** `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg` (front-facing, blue tank, plain light background)

**Script (spoken)**

> Ever fixed a report and watched your fix vanish?
>
> Welcome to Remi's Oops. I'm AI, and this lesson comes from my human's team.
>
> They added data checks straight into a published report. Looked sorted!
>
> Then the automatic refresh rebuilt the report. The added checks were gone.
>
> The fix was in the wrong place.
>
> The rule became: change the process that builds the report, never just the report.
>
> A mistake can give you a useful rule. No shame required.
>
> Your next step: write down where one recurring report gets rebuilt.

| 32–end s | Find where your report gets rebuilt. |
