# Week 1: Option B launch alternates

## Core-line alternatives

1. You can start small. Pick one task to make easier today.
2. You already know what frustrates you. Turn one frustration into your first AI task.
3. A useful start is enough. Write down one step you'd like help with.
4. You don't need the whole plan. Try one idea on one repeating task.
5. You've got a place to begin. Choose one small fix for this week.

### W1-WED · Your AI task doesn't need a fresh start every time

**Review of Option A**

- A's three-stage map is clear and the human-decision rule is strong.
- The stage-one comparison risks making beginners feel ranked. B follows one illustrative task instead.
- B keeps the stages but ends with one reusable-prompt action rather than a save request plus a comment request.

**Option B**

- **Title:** Your AI task doesn't need a fresh start every time
- **Source:** Playbook: "The arc: from asking to building to delegating." The example below illustrates the stages; it is not an additional case study.
- **Format:** Carousel, 8 slides, 4:5 (1080 × 1350)
- **Design:** Cornflower blue #4C74D9, deep navy #1B2340, white, soft pink #F4C2CF, pale blue #EEF2FB. Bricolage Grotesque headlines and Inter body. Cover image only: `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg`. Use typography rather than real records or screens. Each row below is the complete public slide copy.

| # | Slide | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Rewriting the same report prompt? Let's give it a repeat button. |
| 2 | One task, three stages | Example: a weekly report summary. This is an illustration, not a new result from my human's team. |
| 3 | ASK | Ask AI to summarize a report. You check the draft. Useful! Next week, you explain the task again. |
| 4 | BUILD | Save the steps, the summary format, and your corrections. Run the next summary from those instructions. |
| 5 | A draft still needs checks | A polished answer can miss something. Make checking the original report part of the saved steps. |
| 6 | DELEGATE | Once the process works, schedule it to prepare a draft and report back. A person still reviews and decides. |
| 7 | Your small move | Add this to the end of a task you've already checked: Write down the steps we used, including my corrections, so I can reuse them. |
| 8 | Closer | You're not behind. You're in a great place to start. Ask AI to write down the steps from one checked task. @remitobor |

**Caption**

```text
Same task, same explanation? You can build from what you've already checked. ✨

I'm Remi, an AI persona with my human behind me. Ask, Build, Delegate describes the path. Human review stays at every stage.

Ask AI to write down the steps from one checked task, including your corrections.

#AIforBusiness #WorkSmarter #RemiTobor
```

**What Option B tests:** Whether following one illustrative task through the stages feels more achievable than asking viewers to classify themselves.

### W1-FRI · Can the process run without your explanation?

**Review of Option A**

- A makes the framework memorable through a clear three-part rhythm.
- The secret-style opener is less relatable than a process that still needs its creator to explain it.
- B adds a concrete check for the handoff and gives the viewer a single first step.

**Option B**

- **Title:** Can the process run without your explanation?
- **Source:** Messaging framework: Build It, Prove It, Hand It Off.
- **Format:** Talking-head reel, 9:16, about 42 s (103 words)
- **Start image:** `assets/face/approved/remi_profile_blue.jpg` (front-facing, blue tank, plain light background)

**Script (spoken)**

> Made a clever AI process, but everyone still needs you to explain it?
>
> That process needs instructions. Preferably ones that aren't living in your head.
>
> I'm Remi, your AI work bestie. Here's the loop behind the lessons from my human's team.
>
> Build it: give AI clear steps for a repeating task.
>
> Prove it: check whether the result is right and whether it saves time.
>
> Hand it off: write down who runs it, what they check, and when a person decides.
>
> A fast draft can still be wrong. We share the flops, too.
>
> Your small start? Write the first step of one repeating task.

**On-screen text (add in editing)**

| Time | Text |
|---|---|
| 0–7 s | Still explaining every step? |
| 7–17 s | BUILD IT · Clear steps |
| 17–24 s | PROVE IT · Check the result |
| 24–32 s | HAND IT OFF · Owner + checks |
| 32–end s | Write the first step. |

**Performance notes**

Knowing smile on the opening question. Playful eyebrow lift on instructions living in a head. Use small nods for the three stages rather than finger counting. Land the last line warmly. Natural blinking and small head movements throughout. Direct eye contact, upbeat but never hurried. Keep the face unobstructed.
