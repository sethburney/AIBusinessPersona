# Messaging Framework: "Build It, Prove It, Hand It Off"

> **Status:** Draft v1 (2026-11-04). Built from the owner's six real implementations. Every **[Confirm]** item needs a true detail from the owner before a post goes live. No results, numbers, or tool names are invented here.

## 1. The core message

**Remi's promise to business leaders:**
> AI won't make you efficient by itself. Systems do. Build it once, prove it works, then hand it to your team so it runs without you.

Every post on these topics teaches one piece of the same loop:

| Step | What it means | What Remi teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Build it** | Set up the process, using AI where it saves real time | The actual steps, tools by category, and prompts |
| **2. Prove it** | Track the few numbers that show whether it works | What to measure, how to A/B test, when to kill an idea |
| **3. Hand it off** | Document it so the team can run and repeat it | SOPs, checklists, owners, and review rhythm |

**Why this works for Remi:** It turns "my human's" real implementations into teachable stories (canon fact: her real-world knowledge comes from her human). It fits "Real stories only" and "Every tip human-checked." The leader angle ("so it runs without you") gives every topic the same ending, which builds recognition.

## 2. Who this is for

This narrows the audience from "anyone at work" to people who own outcomes:

- **Primary:** Business owners and leaders of small to mid-size teams who run sales and marketing, or oversee the people who do.
- **Secondary:** Sales ops, marketing ops, and marketing managers who do the hands-on setup.

**Their pains (to confirm with real audience feedback later):** messy CRM data, outreach that doesn't scale, leads that never convert, content that doesn't get found, no clear numbers, and processes that live in one person's head.

## 3. Story format for every implementation

Each post or series follows this arc, which keeps posts consistent and honest:

1. **The mess:** What was broken before. *(real detail)*
2. **What my human's team did:** The fix, step by step.
3. **Where AI helped:** The specific job AI did, and what it couldn't do.
4. **How we knew it worked:** The metric watched. *(real result, or "still testing")*
5. **How it got handed off:** The SOP, owner, and review rhythm.
6. **Your move:** One action the viewer can take this week.

## 4. The six topics

### Topic 1: CRM sync and contact cleansing

- **Key message:** Your outreach is only as good as your data. Clean, synced contacts come before any automation.
- **Leader angle:** Bad data quietly wastes your team's time and makes every report wrong.
- **Post ideas:**
  - "The 5 signs your CRM data is costing you deals" (carousel)
  - "How my human's team synced [Confirm: systems] without losing contacts" (talking-head reel)
  - "Using AI to find duplicates and fix messy fields" (screen-share style reel)
  - "Remi's Oops: what went wrong during the sync" (if there's a real mistake to share)
  - "The monthly data-hygiene checklist we hand to the team" (carousel, saves well)
- **Digital product tie-in:** CRM cleanup checklist and data-hygiene SOP template.
- **[Confirm]:** Which CRM and connected systems; how many contacts or what kind of mess; what "cleansing" covered (duplicates, formatting, enrichment, bounced emails); before/after result; who runs it now and how often.

### Topic 2: Sequence building and automated outreach

- **Key message:** Automation should sound like a person, not a robot. Build sequences that are personal at scale.
- **Leader angle:** One good sequence, tested and documented, replaces hours of manual follow-up every week.
- **Post ideas:**
  - "Anatomy of a sequence that gets replies" (carousel)
  - "Flip It: a manual follow-up mess turned into a 5-step sequence" (before/after reel)
  - "How I use AI to draft sequence emails (and what my human always rewrites)" (reel)
  - "When NOT to automate" (talking head; builds trust)
  - "The sequence handoff doc: owner, triggers, exit rules, review dates" (carousel)
- **Digital product tie-in:** Sequence templates and a prompt pack for outreach drafts.
- **[Confirm]:** Platform used; number and type of steps; channels (email, calls, social); reply or meeting rates before/after if shareable; any deliverability lessons.

### Topic 3: MQL email marketing strategy

- **Key message:** Not every lead is ready. Define what "marketing qualified" means, then nurture people until they are.
- **Leader angle:** A shared MQL definition stops the sales-versus-marketing blame game.
- **Post ideas:**
  - "What actually makes a lead an MQL (and who decides)" (talking head)
  - "Building a lead-scoring sheet with AI in 10 minutes" (screen-share reel)
  - "The 3 nurture emails every MQL track needs" (carousel)
  - "How we hand MQLs to sales without dropping them" (reel)
- **Digital product tie-in:** MQL definition worksheet and nurture email templates.
- **[Confirm]:** How MQL was defined; scoring criteria; email platform; what changed in conversion or sales feedback; how the handoff to sales works.

### Topic 4: Geo-targeted, customized content

- **Key message:** Local content wins when it's actually useful to the local reader, not just a city name swapped in.
- **Leader angle:** A repeatable template lets you scale location content without lowering quality.
- **Post ideas:**
  - "City-swap pages vs. genuinely local pages" (Flip It before/after)
  - "How we use AI to draft local content, and the human check that keeps it accurate" (reel)
  - "What goes into a local page template" (carousel)
  - "How we check location content is still relevant every quarter" (reel)
- **Digital product tie-in:** Local page template and a content-relevance checklist.
- **[Confirm]:** Number of locations or pages; what local details were included; how accuracy was checked; results in traffic or leads if shareable.
- **Caution:** Thin, near-duplicate location pages can hurt search performance. Verify current Google guidance before posting specific claims.

### Topic 5: SEO and AEO foundations

- **Key message:** Getting found takes more than publishing. Pages need to be linked, optimized, indexed, and written so both search engines and AI answer engines can understand them.
- **Leader angle:** A checklist the team runs on every page beats a one-time SEO project.
- **What AEO means (for captions):** Answer Engine Optimization: structuring content so AI tools that answer questions (for example ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews) can find, understand, and cite it.
- **Post ideas:**
  - "Interlinking explained in 30 seconds" (reel)
  - "Backlinks: what we did and what we stopped doing" (talking head)
  - "Images and videos that help your SEO: file names, alt text, size, captions" (carousel)
  - "Is Google even seeing your page? How to check indexing" (screen-share reel)
  - "SEO vs. AEO: what changes when an AI answers the question" (talking head)
  - "The pre-publish checklist our team uses on every page" (carousel, saves well)
- **Digital product tie-in:** SEO/AEO pre-publish checklist.
- **[Confirm]:** Which practices were actually used; tools used; any measurable changes.
- **Caution:** Search guidance changes often. Every technical claim gets checked against current Google Search Central documentation, with the date checked, before posting (see `GUARDRAILS.md`).

### Topic 6: Tracking SEO/AEO and A/B testing at scale

- **Key message:** You can't improve what you don't measure. Track visibility, test one change at a time, and scale what wins.
- **Leader angle:** A simple dashboard and a testing rhythm let you grow content output without guessing.
- **Post ideas:**
  - "The 5 numbers we watch weekly" (carousel)
  - "How we track whether AI answer engines mention us" (reel)
  - "Scaling page output without losing quality: our workflow" (talking head)
  - "A/B testing 101: one change, one metric, enough time" (reel)
  - "Win Wall / Oops: a test that surprised us" (only real results)
  - "Handing the dashboard to the team: who checks what, when" (carousel)
- **Digital product tie-in:** Tracking dashboard template and an A/B test planning sheet.
- **[Confirm]:** How AEO visibility is tracked (manual checks, a tool, or both); dashboard tool; what was tested; page volume before/after; results that are shareable.

## 5. The thread through everything: efficient leadership

Every topic ends with the same three leader questions, which can also become their own recurring series (**"The Handoff"**):

1. **What's the one number that tells you it's working?**
2. **What would break if you were out for two weeks?**
3. **Who owns it, and what's their checklist?**

## 7. Message guardrails for these topics

- **Keep the organization anonymous.** It's "my human's team." No company name, clients, coworkers, or internal screenshots that identify them.
- **Get permission for real numbers.** Share results only if the owner is comfortable they're not confidential to the organization or its clients. **[Confirm]** with the owner and, if needed, the organization.
- **Tools by category first.** Name specific tools only when it's useful and accurate; never imply a sponsorship that doesn't exist. Paid mentions always get disclosed.
- **Verify technical claims.** SEO, AEO, and email deliverability guidance changes. Check current official documentation before posting.
- **Mark guesses.** If something is a belief rather than a measured result, the post says so.
